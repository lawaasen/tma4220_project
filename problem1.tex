\section*{\textcolor{teal}{\texttt{Weak formulation of the Poisson problem}}}
Choose a test function $v \in H^1(\Omega)$ with $v=0$ on
$\partial\Omega_D$. Multiplying $-\Delta u=f$ by $v$ and
integrating by parts gives
\[
    \int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \, dx
    = \int_\Omega fv \, dx
    + \int_{\partial\Omega} (\nabla u \cdot n)v \, ds.\]
The integral over $\partial\Omega_D$ vanishes because $v=0$ there.
On the two Neumann boundaries, the normal derivatives are $g_{N,1}$
and $g_{N,2}$. On the Robin boundary, the normal derivative is
$\kappa(g_R-u)$. Substitute:
\[\begin{aligned}
    \int_{\partial\Omega} (\nabla u \cdot n)v \, ds
    &= \int_{\partial\Omega_{N,1}} g_{N,1}v \, ds
     + \int_{\partial\Omega_{N,2}} g_{N,2}v \, ds \\
    &\quad + \int_{\partial\Omega_R} \kappa(g_R-u)v \, ds.
\end{aligned}\]
Move the term with $\kappa uv$ to the left.
Now the weak formulation is: Find $u \in H^1_{g_D,\partial\Omega_D}(\Omega)$
such that, for every $v \in H^1_{0,\partial\Omega_D}(\Omega)$,
\[\begin{aligned}
    \int_\Omega \nabla u \cdot \nabla v \, dx
    + \int_{\partial\Omega_R} \kappa uv \, ds
    &= \int_\Omega fv \, dx
     + \int_{\partial\Omega_{N,1}} g_{N,1}v \, ds \\
    &\quad + \int_{\partial\Omega_{N,2}} g_{N,2}v \, ds
     + \int_{\partial\Omega_R} \kappa g_Rv \, ds.
\end{aligned}\]
